FLEXO Plate travel: the maths

What the simulator calculates, as a whole and across the face, the constants it uses, how sensitive the result is to each of them, and which parts outside sources confirm. Every worked figure uses the simulator's 71 tooth example on the standard stack, and was produced by the simulator's own functions.

Confirmed outside

The gear, the print diameter, the distortion formula, the bare cylinder diameter and the expansion coefficients. Ten published worked examples and two published tables of 50 values were reworked with the simulator's functions, and all agree.

Worked out here

How far the print moves when the cylinder, tape or plate is off the standard. It follows from the confirmed geometry and reduces to the published formula at the standard, but no source was found that states these figures, and they have not been checked on a press. The same goes for everything across the face (section 6), for which no source was looked for.

Assumed

A rigid stack, a gear driven press, and only the cylinder growing with heat. Across the face, a plate that is either fully stiff or fully giving. Section 9 lists what that leaves out.

1. The model in six lines

A gear driven press lays one repeat down per turn of the plate cylinder gear, whatever the cylinder under the plate measures. The plate's polyester backing does not stretch, so the art keeps its made length at the backing. The gear lays it down at the pitch line.

Symbols: N teeth, CP circular pitch, R repeat, rp pitch radius, tp plate, tb plate backing, tt mounting tape, K the amount the plate is made short, L the plate's flat length, rb the radius of the backing line, P what prints.

R = N × CP
71 × 0.125 = 8.8750"

rp = R ÷ 2π
8.8750 ÷ 6.28319 = 1.41250"  (pitch diameter 2.8250")
Confirmed The pitch circle is the printing circle: its circumference is the repeat.
K = 2π (tp − tb)
6.28319 × (0.067 − 0.005) = 0.3896"
Confirmed The plate thickness constant, or K factor. Only the soft part of the plate stretches when it is wrapped.

Standard BCD = 2 (rp − tt − tp)
2 × (1.41250 − 0.015 − 0.067) = 2.6610"
Confirmed The bare cylinder that puts the plate's face on the pitch line: the print diameter less twice the plate and tape. See the squeeze note in section 9.
P = L × rp ÷ rb    with   rb = BCD ÷ 2 + tt + tb

Worked out here What prints. The art spans an angle of L ÷ rb on the cylinder, and the gear moves the web rp for each unit of angle. On the standard cylinder this gives back exactly R, which is the published distortion formula read the other way round.

Travel is P − R: negative prints short, positive prints long. In the simulator these lines are the functions gear() and printed().

2. How each factor enters

All four factors act through one mechanism: they move the backing line, or change the length of the art on it. Near the standard, lifting the backing line by a small amount Δr moves the end of the repeat by

travel ≈ −(2π ÷ scale) × Δr
6.28319 ÷ 0.95611 = 6.572 per unit of radius
Worked out here So 0.001" of extra radius under the plate prints the 71 tooth repeat 6.57 thousandths short. The 2π is the same one that is in K: one more thousandth of radius is 6.28 more thousandths of circumference.

6. Across the face

The simulator also lets the cylinder, the tape and the plate differ at four readings across the face, and shows what that does to the sheet. Nothing in this section comes from an outside source, and none of it has been checked on a press.

Symbols: W the plate width; xi where reading i sits, as a share of the width from the middle: −½, −⅙, +⅙ and +½, so the four are evenly spaced from one edge of the plate to the other; bi, τi and pi what the BCD, the tape and the plate are off by at reading i; ri the radius of the backing line there.

ri = [ (BCD + bi) (1 + α ΔT) ÷ 2 + tt + τi + tb ] × L ÷ (L − 2π pi)
tape +0.001" at reading 4:
1.35050" 1.35050" 1.35050" 1.35150"
Worked out here The backing line at each reading: the cylinder as it measures there, with the tape as it is there. The last factor is the plate. A plate that is p thicker at a reading is counted the way a thicker plate is counted as a whole in section 2: its art is 2πp shorter there. That is written as the radius that prints the same length, so the three kinds of reading can be handled as one. On the standard stack L = 2π rb exactly, so 0.001" of plate comes to 0.001" of radius, the same as tape, to within 0.1%. Section 9 says why this line is a choice and not geometry.

The plate, two ways

How a real plate takes four radii that are not in line depends on how stiff it is, which the simulator does not know. It shows the two limits, and a real plate lies between them.

Follows:   Pi = L × rp ÷ ri
travel 0, 0, 0 and −6.567 thou
Worked out here The plate gives enough to sit on the cylinder as it is. Each reading prints its own length, by the last line of section 1, and nothing moves sideways.
Stiff:   r̄ = (r1 + r2 + r3 + r4) ÷ 4    s = Σ xi ri ÷ Σ xi²
r̄ = 1.35075"   s = 0.00090"
Worked out here The plate will not stretch, so it cannot follow four radii that are not in line. It sits on the straight taper nearest all four, by least squares. s is how much larger that line's radius is at edge 4 than at edge 1. Σ xi² is 5⁄9, so a reading at an edge counts nine tenths of itself toward the taper and an inner reading three tenths. Every reading counts a quarter toward r̄.
Pi = L × rp ÷ (r̄ + s xi)
travel +1.315, −0.657, −2.628 and −4.598 thou
Worked out here The same line of section 1, on the fitted taper. The tail edge stays straight and leans.
sideways at the tail = L² × (s ÷ W) ÷ (2 r̄)
8.4854² × (0.00090 ÷ 13) ÷ (2 × 1.35075) = 0.00185"
Worked out here A cylinder with a straight taper is a slice of a cone whose point lies r̄ W ÷ s away along the face. Unrolled flat, the lines that run round the cone become arcs about that point. A flat sheet laid square to one of them at the lead edge runs straight on and leaves the arc by length² ÷ (2 × that distance), toward the large end. Because L is close to 2π r̄, this is close to 2π² r̄ s ÷ W.

The four figures over the sheet

The plate is laid down at the lead edge, so travel is measured from there and the lead corners read zero.

  • Out of square is the travel at reading 4 less the travel at reading 1.
  • Diagonals differ is √((R + travel4)² + (W + sideways)²) less √((R + travel1)² + (W − sideways)²): lead corner 1 to tail corner 4, less lead corner 4 to tail corner 1.
  • Tail edge bow is how far readings 2 and 3 stand off the straight line from reading 1 to reading 4 at the tail. The larger of the two is shown, and anything under 0.005 thou is shown as straight.
  • Tail sideways is the line above. It is zero when the plate follows.

8. How to check the part that is worked out here

The off-standard travel is geometry, not a measurement. Two press tests would confirm or correct it:

  • A known shim. Print a repeat, then add a film of known thickness under the mounting tape and print again with nothing else changed. At 71 teeth the model predicts 6.6 thou shorter per 0.001" of film, measured over one full repeat.
  • Two measured cylinders. Mount the same plate, on the same tape, on two cylinders of one tooth count whose BCD differs by a known amount. The model predicts 3.3 thou of print length per 0.001" of BCD, so cylinders 0.0008" apart should differ by about 2.6 thou at the end of the repeat.
  • A known shim at one edge. For across the face: put a film of known thickness under the tape at one edge of the plate only, print, and measure the repeat at both edges of the sheet and how far a tail mark sits sideways from its lead mark. For 0.001" at one edge reading, at 71 teeth on a 13" plate, the model's two limits are 5.9 to 6.6 thou out of square and 0 to 1.8 thou of walk toward the thick edge. Where a real plate falls between them is what this test would show.

If the press shows less than predicted, the likely cause is the soft plate or the foam tape giving at the nip, which the model does not include.

9. What the model leaves out

  • Assumed A rigid stack. The plate and tape are taken at their nominal thickness. On press the plate is squeezed into the web by a few thousandths, and foam tape compresses. One source gives the lightest "kiss" engagement as 0.003" [1].
  • Assumed No squeeze allowance in the standard BCD. Some cylinder makers size the bare cylinder 0.004" larger than the plain formula, 0.002" of squeeze each side, so the plate's face sits slightly proud of the pitch line. The forum example in section 7 shows exactly this [6]. A shop whose cylinders were bought that way should expect its measured BCDs to sit about 0.004" above the simulator's standard.
  • Assumed A gear driven press. The web moves one repeat per turn of the gear and does not slip, stretch or shrink. On a servo press the repeat is set by the drive, not by a pitch line. A plate face that is off the print diameter also slurs [1], which the model does not draw.
  • Assumed Only the cylinder grows with heat. The gear, the plate, the tape and the web are left at their sizes, and the cylinder is at one even temperature. No flexo source was found that gives a temperature figure for print length, so this row rests on handbook expansion alone.
  • Assumed The backing is the line that keeps its length. This is the convention behind every distortion formula cited here [1] [2] [4]. One supplier states the constant with the photopolymer thickness alone [3], which is the same thing when the backing is the only other layer.

Across the face (section 6) adds these:

  • Assumed The plate is one of two limits. Stiff or Follows, with no stiffness figure anywhere. A real plate lies between them, and the model does not say where.
  • Assumed A thick spot in the plate prints short there. By the geometry of section 1 alone, a plate that is thicker at one reading would not change the length at all: the backing line does not move, and the art was made for the plate as a whole. The simulator instead counts a plate reading the way it counts the plate as a whole, so that all three kinds of reading move the print. This is a choice made for the simulator, not a result.
  • Assumed Four readings, evenly spaced, edge to edge of the plate, and for the stiff plate a straight line fitted through them by least squares. Another fit would share the readings out differently.
  • Assumed The plate is laid square at the lead edge. All of the walk then shows at the tail. Laid from the middle of its length, the same taper would move each end a quarter as far.
  • Assumed Nothing else changes across the face. Impression, the web and the temperature are taken as even from side to side.
